6. Why P2P Is Important

P2P is not included merely to make the product resemble another financial platform.

It addresses a specific class of interactions: transactions in which users need to find counterparties and follow a defined process for exchanging value.

A P2P marketplace can provide structured offers, order records, transaction states, participant communication, and dispute-handling procedures.

These functions can help reduce reliance on informal arrangements.

For example, a buyer may want to compare available offers rather than depend on a single quoted rate. A seller may want clearer rules about the conditions under which an order is completed. Both participants may benefit from a record of the transaction's progress.

However, these benefits depend on the actual operating model.

P2P does not automatically make a transaction safe, guarantee that a counterparty will fulfill an agreement, or eliminate payment disputes.

The platform must define its role and responsibilities clearly.

7. The Platform's Role as an Intermediary

The intended Nexa model includes providing digital infrastructure through which users can interact and conduct supported transactions.

In a marketplace, the platform may connect buyers and independent sellers.

In a P2P workflow, it may help users discover offers, create orders, follow defined rules, and access a structured dispute process.

The objective is to organize interactions and provide a coherent system for recording relevant events.

This design also requires a clear distinction between the responsibilities of participants and those of the platform.

However, describing Nexa as an intermediary is not a way to bypass legal obligations.

The platform's actual obligations depend on what it does, which assets or payment methods it supports, whether it controls or holds funds, the countries in which it operates, and the applicable regulatory framework.

Some activities may require licensed partners, specific authorization, identity verification, consumer-protection controls, or other compliance measures.

The final operating model must be evaluated on its substance rather than its label.

The intention is to build a useful platform with clearly defined responsibilities and an operating model that can meet the requirements applicable to its services.

8. Why Escrow Requires Careful Design

Escrow-style workflows are intended to provide conditional handling of eligible transactions.

A typical example is a marketplace order in which the buyer's funds or assets are held or restricted until the required transaction conditions have been satisfied.

This may help establish clearer expectations for both parties.

But an escrow system must answer important questions:

- Who actually holds or controls the relevant assets?
- What conditions permit their release?
- How are cancellations and refunds processed?
- What happens when a participant fails to respond?
- How are disputes reviewed?
- How are conflicting or duplicate requests prevented from producing inconsistent results?
- What records are retained for reconciliation and review?

A database status alone does not establish that funds are protected.

The implementation must match the claims made about the service, and the custody model must satisfy the applicable legal requirements.
